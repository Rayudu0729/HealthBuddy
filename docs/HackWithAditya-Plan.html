Overview

We build an always-on AI companion wearable (under Rs 5,000) that talks in Telugu or Hindi, predicts and prevents falls, detects emergencies, tracks location and alerts caregivers.

  • Track: HealthTech & Bio-Innovation (also eligible for the Hardware Innovation prize)
  • Target users: elderly people living alone, and rural patients cared for by ASHA workers

System architecture

The wearable sends readings over Wi-Fi to the backend, which feeds the AI layer and the caregiver app; an SOS also goes straight out by SMS so alerts work without internet.

The AI layer (highlighted) is what makes this new: it turns daily motion and vitals into fall-risk warnings with causes, and runs the voice agent.

Voice agent on the wearable

  1. Wake word ("Hey Saathi") runs on the ESP32-S3 (Espressif ESP-SR / WakeNet, or Picovoice Porcupine), or a fall or risk event wakes the agent.
  2. Speech goes to Sarvam AI or Bhashini for Telugu or Hindi speech-to-text.
  3. An LLM agent with tools decides what to do: get_vitals, call_family, send_sos, log_medicine.
  4. The reply is spoken back in Telugu through the speaker.
  5. The device can also speak first: stand-up guard, "Are you okay?" after a fall, medicine reminders.

Battery strategy: listen continuously only in waking hours; at night wake on motion; SOS button as a push-to-talk fallback; offload heavy AI to the family phone over Bluetooth when nearby; charge nightly like a smartwatch (estimate, measure in Week 4).

Firmware: SaathiOS

SaathiOS is our own health-first runtime built on FreeRTOS + ESP-IDF: fall detection and SOS always come first, even at 5% battery. We do not write a kernel; FreeRTOS gives us a proven scheduler and Espressif's Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ESP-SR and ESP-ADF need it.

We build the top two layers; FreeRTOS, ESP-IDF and the hardware are existing parts.

SaathiOS serviceWhat it does
Health-priority schedulerFall detection, SOS and voice always pre-empt other tasks; sync waits
Event busModules publish and subscribe to events instead of calling each other
Adaptive power managerChanges sensor rates and listening windows by time of day, motion and risk
Safety modeOn low battery or a fault, keeps only fall detection and SOS running
Skill systemVoice skills (vitals, medicine, call family) plug in without touching the core
Fail-safe OTAFirmware updates over Wi-Fi with automatic rollback if the new version crashes

Events: FALL, STAND_UP, RISK_HIGH, WAKE_WORD, SOS_BUTTON, MISSED_CALL, LOW_BATTERY.

Power modes

ModeWhenWhat runs
DayWaking hoursAll sensors, wake word listening, regular sync

FreeRTOS tools we use: queues for sensor data, event groups for alerts, a mutex on the GSM module, a task watchdog that restarts a hung task.

Firmware folders (one owner each): sensors/, fall_detect/, voice/, alerts/, sync/, power/, plus saathios/ for the core services.

Kernel setup for the prototype

FreeRTOS through ESP-IDF v5.x is the kernel: it is built in, dual-core, and the only option that keeps ESP-SR, ESP-ADF, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth working.

Pitch outline (Member 3)

Ten slides for a 4-minute demo, which is the finalist format; open with a real story, show the device working live, and close on cost and reach.

  1. Hook: a real story from the interviews ("My grandmother lay on the floor for 6 hours…").
  2. Problem: elderly people living alone, rural areas, help arrives late; use cited figures only.
  3. Why current devices fall short: react after the fall, silent, need internet, cost Rs 30,000+, English only.
  4. Our solution: "Other devices call for help after you fall. Ours stops the fall." Product photo.
  5. Live demo 1: a judge stands up fast; the device says "Sit for 30 seconds" in Telugu.
  6. Live demo 2: "Hey Saathi, how is my heart rate?", then "Bachao" triggers an SOS on the family phone.
  7. Live demo 3: unplug Wi-Fi, fake a fall, SMS arrives; a missed call returns location and vitals.
  8. How it works: the architecture diagram; privacy (on-device wake word, no camera).
  9. Test results: falls detected, false alarms, wake-word accuracy, battery life, from our own test log.
  10. Impact, cost and next steps: under Rs 5,000; ASHA-worker network; pilot with a local old-age home or PHC.

Judging criteria to hit: problem relevance, prototype credibility, feasibility, clarity, originality.

Demo tips: rehearse the live demo 10+ times; keep a recorded video as a backup if Wi-Fi or GSM fails on stage.
